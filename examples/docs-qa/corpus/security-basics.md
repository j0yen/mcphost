# Security basics

Baseline security practices every employee at Acorn Robotics is expected
to follow, independent of role or team.

## Passwords

Passwords must be at least fourteen characters and must not reuse any of
your last ten passwords. Employees must rotate their password every
ninety days; the identity provider sends a reminder seven days before
expiry and locks the account if the deadline passes without a rotation.
A password manager is provided to every employee and is the recommended
way to generate and store passwords -- writing passwords in a notes app is
against policy.

## Two-factor authentication

Two-factor authentication is mandatory on the identity provider, email,
and the source control host. A hardware security key is recommended and
provided on request; an authenticator app is an acceptable alternative.
SMS-based two-factor is disabled by policy because it is vulnerable to
SIM-swap attacks.

## Device security

Laptops must have disk encryption enabled (enabled by default on
company-imaged machines) and must lock automatically after five minutes
of inactivity. Do not disable the automatic screen lock even temporarily;
security scans flag disabled screen locks and open a ticket with your
manager.

## Phishing

Report suspected phishing emails using the "Report Phishing" button in the
mail client rather than forwarding them, which strips the headers security
needs to investigate. Acorn Robotics runs periodic simulated phishing
tests; clicking a simulated link triggers a short, mandatory training
module, not a disciplinary action.

## Data classification

Data is classified public, internal, confidential, or restricted.
Restricted data (customer personal information, unreleased hardware
designs) may only be stored in approved systems, never in personal cloud
storage or a personal email account. Confidential data may be shared
internally but not externally without a signed agreement.

## Reporting a security concern

Report a suspected security incident (a lost device, a suspicious login,
a possible data exposure) to #security immediately, day or night; this
channel is monitored around the clock, unlike most internal channels. Do
not wait for business hours if you suspect an active compromise.

## Common questions

**How often must employees rotate their password?** Every ninety days.

**Is SMS-based two-factor authentication allowed?** No, it is disabled by
policy in favor of a hardware key or an authenticator app.

**What should I do if I click a phishing link by accident?** Report it to
#security immediately; the sooner it's reported, the smaller the blast
radius.
